## What your 2002 RHD Fit most likely is

If yours is a **right-hand-drive 2002 Honda Fit**, rather than a European-market Jazz, the most likely origin is the Japanese-market first-generation Fit. The first-generation Fit began Japanese production in **June 2001**, and Honda's Japanese VIN/model codes were:

| Model code | Engine | Drive | Relevance to a 2002 RHD car |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GD1** | L13A 1.3 i-DSI | 2WD | **Most likely** |
| **GD2** | L13A 1.3 i-DSI | 4WD | Possible; Japan-only |
| **GD3** | L15A 1.5 VTEC | 2WD | Possible, but production began Sept. 2002 |
| **GD4** | L15A 1.5 VTEC | 4WD | Possible, but production began Sept. 2002 |

Honda's own production/recall records show GD1 and GD2 entering production in June 2001, while GD3/GD4 appear from September 2002.  Honda Japan+1

So if your car is simply described as a **2002 Fit**, is RHD, and isn't a 1.5 VTEC or 4WD version, **GD1 with the L13A engine is the leading identification**.

The important distinction is that **"2002" is not enough to establish GD1**. The chassis/model code on the identification plate or VIN will settle it.

## The Mitsubishi alternator connection is real

This is the particularly interesting part of your question.

Honda's Japanese parts catalog for an actual **LA-GD1 / L13A Fit** identifies the alternator section as:

> **A.C. generator (Mitsubishi)**

and lists:

- Honda OE: **31100-PWA-004**
- Mitsubishi identification: **AHGA56**
- Rotor: 31101-PWA-004
- Stator: 31102-P2E-A01
- Rectifier: 31127-PWA-004
- Regulator: 31150-PWA-004

So this isn't merely an aftermarket cross-reference. The Honda catalog itself identifies the generator as **Mitsubishi**.  PartSouq+1

A second Japanese parts catalog for GD1 gives the same combination: **31100-PWA-004 / AHGA56 / Mitsubishi**.  PartSouq

That makes your description — **"Mitsubishi's OEM alternator, installed by the Honda factory"** — essentially correct.

## The really useful timeline

The alternator appears to have been a **shared Mitsubishi unit across a surprisingly broad portion of the first-generation L-series Honda lineup**.

The early unit is:

**Honda 31100-PWA-004**\
↔ **Mitsubishi A5TB0091 / A005TB0091**\
↔ **Honda AHGA56**

It is an **80 A, 12/14 V, 5-groove-pulley** alternator.  DAH KEE Co., Ltd.+1

And the application data is especially interesting:

- **Fit/Jazz GD1 1.3 L13A**
- **Fit/Jazz GD2 1.3 L13A**
- **Fit/Jazz GD3 1.5 L15A**
- **Fit/Jazz GD4 1.5 L15A**
- Honda City GD-series derivatives
- Honda Mobilio
- later Honda Civic 1.4
- Honda Airwave

The cross-reference data identifies the same 31100-PWA-004/AHGA56 family on both the **GD1 1.3** and **GD3 1.5** Fit.  K Motorshop+1

There is particularly good Japanese rebuild-catalog evidence showing:

- GD2 / L13A: **2001–2005 period → 31100-PWA-004 / AHGA56**
- GD3 / L15A: **2002–2005 period → 31100-PWA-004 / AHGA56**
- GD4 / L15A: **2002–2005 period → 31100-PWA-004 / AHGA56**

That strongly suggests Honda standardized this Mitsubishi alternator across the early GD chassis rather than giving the 1.3 and 1.5 engines completely different generators.  Nissan Parts Ibaraki

### Then came the later Mitsubishi version

The later replacement/evolution is:

**Honda 31100-RSH-004**\
↔ **Mitsubishi A5TB1391 / A005TB1391**\
↔ **AHGA69**

The earlier **31100-PWA-004 / AHGA56** and later **31100-RSH-004 / AHGA69** are extensively cross-referenced as the same general alternator family.  K Motorshop+1

A New Era Japanese alternator catalog is particularly helpful here: it identifies **31100-RSH-004 / A005TB1391 / 31100-PWK-Q01** with **2007–08 Honda Jazz/Fit**, while also listing 31100-PWA-004 as the earlier generator reference.  New Era

So, schematically:

**2001/02 → \~2005**

- Mitsubishi **AHGA56**
- Mitsubishi **A5TB0091**
- Honda **31100-PWA-004**

**\~2005 onward / later revisions**

- Mitsubishi **AHGA69**
- Mitsubishi **A5TB1391**
- Honda **31100-RSH-004**
- later related Honda numbers including **31100-PWK-Q01**

There are some market/engine/date overlaps, so I would **not use 2005 as a hard universal changeover date**. The actual alternator fitted to a particular chassis number is more authoritative.

## Why this matters for identifying your car

There's a particularly useful piece of evidence for your situation: a parts catalog listing a **2005 RHD GD3 1.5 VTEC** explicitly shows:

**31100-PWA-004 — Alternator assembly AHGA56 (Mitsubishi).**  Megazip

In other words, the Mitsubishi unit wasn't restricted to the L13A/GD1. It crossed over into the **1.5 L15A/GD3** as well.

Another catalog lists the 31100-PWA-004/AHGA56 combination across GD1, GD2, GD3 and other L-series applications.  Nissan Parts Ibaraki

### Therefore, the alternator cannot by itself distinguish GD1 from GD3.

That's an important conclusion.

If you're trying to determine **which exact model you have**, I'd use this hierarchy:

1. **Chassis/model code** — GD1/GD2/GD3/GD4.
2. **Engine code** — L13A vs L15A.
3. **2WD vs 4WD.**
4. **Transmission/trim.**
5. Alternator part number — useful for confirming the production/component family, but **not sufficient to identify the chassis**.
