[The red battery indicator is not actually lighting up, but sometimes it flickers]

✨
If your red battery indicator light is mostly off but flickering intermittently, it means your car is experiencing a temporary drop in voltage or a loose electrical connection. In a 2002 Honda Fit, this indicator monitors the charging system (the alternator and related wiring), not just the physical health of the battery itself. [1, 2] 
The most common causes for a flickering light in this specific vehicle include:
## 1. Loose or Corroded Battery Terminals

* 
* The Issue: Vibrations from driving cause loose or heavily corroded connections to temporarily break contact.
* What to check: Pop the hood and inspect the positive and negative terminals on the battery. If they can twist by hand, they are too loose. Look for a crusty white or blue powder (corrosion) which acts as an electrical insulator. [1, 3, 4, 5] 
* 

## 2. Failing Alternator Brushes or Voltage Regulator

* 
* The Issue: The alternator uses carbon brushes that press against a spinning rotor to generate electricity. As they wear down over time, they begin to bounce or lose constant contact, causing the alternator to cut out for milliseconds at a time. [6] 
* Honda's Dual-Mode Charging: First-generation Fits use a dual-mode charging system controlled by the car's computer. When electricity demands are low (e.g., cruising at idle with no AC on), the car purposely puts the alternator into a "low-output" mode. If the alternator is on its way out or a cheap aftermarket replacement was installed, this transition can trigger an intermittent flicker. [6] 
* 

## 3. A Slipping Serpentine Belt

* 
* The Issue: The alternator is spun by a rubber drive belt connected directly to the engine. If the belt is old, stretched out, or loose, it can slip on the alternator pulley—especially during sudden acceleration or when driving through wet puddles.
* What to check: Inspect the belt for cracks, fraying, or a shiny, glazed appearance on the smooth side. [1, 7, 8] 
* 

## 4. Loose Alternator Control Wiring

* 
* The Issue: On the back of the alternator, there is a small plastic wiring plug containing the wire that controls the dashboard light. If this connector is loose, full of road grime, or has a partially broken wire, it will flicker as you hit bumps. [9, 10] 
*

The EPS light stands for Electric Power Steering. First-generation Honda Fits use an electric motor rather than a hydraulic pump to provide steering assist. When that light illuminates, the computer has detected a fault in the system and will usually disable power steering entirely, making the wheel very heavy and difficult to turn.
Since your battery/alternator light is also flickering, these two issues are highly likely to be connected.
## 1. Low System Voltage (The Most Likely Culprit)

* 
* The Link: Electric power steering systems draw a massive amount of electrical current, especially when you are turning the wheel at low speeds (like parking).
* The Issue: If your alternator is failing or your battery connections are loose (causing that battery light to flicker), the overall voltage in the car drops. When the EPS computer detects that the voltage has dropped below a safe threshold (usually around 11 Volts), it immediately shuts itself off to preserve power for vital engine functions, lighting up the EPS warning.
* 

## 2. A Failing EPS Control Module or Relays

* 
* The Issue: First-generation Honda Fits (GD1/GD3 chassis) are known for occasional solder joint failures inside the EPS control unit or a sticky EPS relay. Over time, heat and vibrations cause these internal connections to crack, causing the system to intermittently lose power and throw the light.
* 

## 3. Faulty Steering Rack Torque Sensor

* 
* The Issue: The EPS system uses a torque sensor on the steering column to measure how hard you are turning the wheel so it knows how much motor assist to provide. If the sensor sends an erratic signal—or if the wiring harness plug near the steering rack is corroded—the system goes into fail-safe mode.

## Step-by-Step Alternator Removal Guide

Because the engine bay of the 1.3L Honda Fit is tightly packed, the alternator is tucked under the intake manifold plumbing on the side of the engine block.
## Tools Needed:

* 
* 10mm, 12mm, and 14mm sockets and wrenches
* Pry bar or long thick screwdriver (for setting belt tension later)
* 

## Removal Steps:

   1. Disconnect the Battery: CRUCIAL STEP. Disconnect the negative (-) battery terminal first. If you do not do this, the live B+ power cable attached to the back of the alternator will arc against the engine block and melt your main wiring harness.
   2. Remove the Drive Belt: Locate the alternator adjustment bracket on top. Loosen the 12mm lock bolt on the slider track, and loosen the 14mm pivot bolt on the bottom of the alternator body. Push the alternator inward toward the engine to create slack, and slide the serpentine belt off the pulley.
   3. Unplug Electrical Connections: Pull the rubber weather boot off the back of the alternator. Remove the 10mm nut holding the heavy B+ power cable. Unclip the green or grey plastic multi-pin wiring harness harness plug.
   4. Remove Mount Bolts: Remove the top adjustment bolt entirely. Then, reach down and extract the long lower pivot bolt.
   5. Wiggle Out the Alternator: The alternator will be tight inside its mounting ears. Use a flathead screwdriver or small pry bar to gently rock it back and forth until it slides free of the engine brackets. Maneuver it upward out of the engine bay.

Installation is the exact reverse. When putting the belt back on, use a pry bar to pull the alternator outward until the belt has about 5-10mm of play, then tighten the slider lock bolt.
